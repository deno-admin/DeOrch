# 19. Job-Specific Generation Workflow

For every new job:

### Step 1 --- Extract

-   Company
-   Role
-   Location
-   Work mode
-   Experience requirement
-   Salary
-   Responsibilities
-   Required skills
-   Application method
-   Recruiter/hiring manager
-   Special questions

### Step 2 --- Match

Choose only relevant experience: - Denovation → current UX/product/brand
work - Vorreix → SaaS/product/design-system experience - Portfolio
project → only when genuinely relevant

### Step 3 --- Draft email

Keep the base structure but rewrite the middle around the job.

### Step 4 --- Draft LinkedIn message

Make it short enough for the connection request and mention the
application when applicable.

### Step 5 --- Answer application questions

Use first person and natural language. Don't turn every form answer into
a cover letter.

### Step 6 --- Verify

Check company, role, recruiter name, dates, claims, salary, portfolio,
and contact details.

## 11. LinkedIn Outreach to Employees / Connections — Opening or Referral Messages

Use these when messaging a person who is already a LinkedIn connection or an employee at a company where I want to work. The goal is to ask naturally whether there are UI/UX/Product Design openings and, if appropriate, whether they could refer me. Keep the tone personal, short, respectful, and direct. Do not sound like mass outreach.

### 10 additional outreach examples

**1. Simple opening + referral**
> Hi [Name], I’m currently exploring UI/UX Designer opportunities and noticed you’re working at [Company]. I wanted to check if there are any openings for UI/UX or Product Design roles. If there’s a suitable opportunity, I’d really appreciate a referral. I can share my resume and portfolio.

**2. Friendly connection message**
> Hi [Name], hope you’re doing well. I’m currently looking for my next opportunity in UI/UX Design and came across [Company]. Are there any openings for UI/UX Designers at the moment? If yes, I’d be grateful if you could guide me on the right way to apply or refer me.

**3. Short and direct**
> Hi [Name], I’m currently looking for UI/UX Designer roles. Since you’re at [Company], I wanted to ask if your team has any current or upcoming openings. If there’s a relevant role, would you be comfortable referring me? I can send across my portfolio and resume.

**4. For someone in the design team**
> Hi [Name], I noticed you’re part of the design team at [Company]. I’m currently working as a User Experience Designer and exploring new UI/UX opportunities. I wanted to check if your team is hiring designers currently. If there’s a suitable opening, I’d appreciate any guidance or referral.

**5. When there is a known company opening**
> Hi [Name], I came across a UI/UX Designer opening at [Company] and saw that you’re working there. I’m interested in applying and wanted to ask if you know whether the team is still hiring. If you feel my profile is a fit, I’d really appreciate a referral.

**6. Asking without immediately pushing for referral**
> Hi [Name], I’m exploring UI/UX Designer opportunities and [Company] is one of the companies I’m interested in. Do you know if there are any openings for UI/UX or Product Designers right now? If there is, I’d be happy to share my portfolio for your feedback.

**7. Slightly more personalized**
> Hi [Name], I’ve been following some of the work coming out of [Company], and I’m currently looking for a UI/UX opportunity where I can contribute to digital products. Since you’re already working there, I wanted to ask if you’re aware of any relevant openings. I’d really appreciate a referral if my profile fits.

**8. For a recruiter/HR connection**
> Hi [Name], I’m currently exploring UI/UX Designer opportunities and wanted to check if [Company] has any openings for designers. I have experience in UX/UI, Figma, responsive web design, prototyping, and design systems. If there’s a suitable role, could you please let me know or guide me on the application process?

**9. Casual but professional**
> Hi [Name], hope all is well. I’m currently looking for a UI/UX Designer opportunity and wanted to check with you since you’re at [Company]. Are there any openings on your team or elsewhere in the company? If you think my profile could be a fit, I’d really appreciate a referral.

**10. Strongest version for an existing connection**
> Hi [Name], I’m currently looking for a new UI/UX Designer opportunity and wanted to reach out to you directly. I’m working as a User Experience Designer at Denovation, with previous experience as a UI/UX Design Intern at Vorreix. If [Company] has any suitable openings, I’d be grateful if you could let me know or refer me. I can share my resume and portfolio.

### Recommended personalization pattern

For each message, change at least one part based on the person:
- Their company: `[Company]`
- Their team/role: `[Design/Product/Engineering team]`
- A specific job post, if one exists
- Why the company interests me
- Whether I am asking for **information**, **application guidance**, or a **referral**

### Preferred approach

Do not always ask for a referral in the first sentence. A natural sequence is:

1. Mention that I’m exploring UI/UX opportunities.
2. Mention that I noticed they work at the company.
3. Ask whether there are relevant openings.
4. If there is a suitable opening, ask whether they would be comfortable referring me.
5. Offer to send my resume and portfolio.

### If the person replies “Yes, we are hiring”

Follow up:
> Thanks, [Name]! I really appreciate it. I’d be happy to share my resume and portfolio. Please let me know if there’s a specific role I should apply for or if you need anything else from my side.

### If they say “Send me your resume”

> Absolutely, thank you! I’m sharing my resume and portfolio here.  
> Portfolio: https://kumaraguru-dk.framer.website/  
> Please let me know if you need any other details from me.

### If they say there are no openings

> Thanks for letting me know, [Name]. I really appreciate you taking the time to respond. If you come across any UI/UX opportunities in the future, I’d be grateful if you could keep me in mind.
